Quran-Derived Definition

الصِّيَام as defined by the Quran itself:

الصِّيَام is a prescribed spiritual institution (كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ) whose stated purpose is developing تقوى (protective awareness of God). It involves abstaining from food, drink, and marital relations from the white thread of dawn (ٱلْفَجْرِ) until night (ٱلَّيْلِ). It was prescribed for previous communities as well. In the month of Ramadan — chosen because the Quran was revealed in it — fasting is obligatory for those present, with exemptions for illness and travel (to be made up later). Fasting also serves as كفّارة (expiation) for specific violations, always as an alternative for those who cannot meet the primary obligation. As a character trait (الصَّائِمِينَ وَالصَّائِمَاتِ), it represents a habitual quality bridging external worship and internal spiritual states.

Key Findings

Finding 1: Two Distinct Words — صِيَام vs. صَوْم

The Quran uses two different masdar (verbal noun) forms from root ص و م with distinct meanings:

  • صِيَام (فِعَال pattern) — Ritual fasting from food/drink. Used in all 10 occurrences across 7 verses related to religious fasting.
  • صَوْم (فَعْل pattern) — General abstention, specifically from speech. Used ONCE in 19:26 (Maryam), where she is told to eat and drink AND observe صَوْم — proving it cannot mean food-fasting.

This is the most significant morphological finding. Traditional tafsirs and translations generally do not highlight this distinction.

Finding 2: Dual Function of صِيَام

The Quran assigns two distinct roles to صِيَام:

  1. Foundational spiritual practice — The Ramadan institution (2:183-187), aimed at developing تقوى
  2. Expiation mechanism (كفّارة) — An alternative for those unable to meet primary obligations (4:92, 5:89, 5:95, 58:4, 2:196), always introduced with فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ

Detail: 03 - Analysis, Finding 2

Finding 3: The Taqwa Connection

The ONLY explicitly stated purpose of صِيَام is لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ (2:183) — "so that you may develop تقوى." This makes تقوى the single Quranic answer to "why fast?" All other attributed purposes (self-discipline, empathy, health) are traditional additions not found in the fasting verses.

Detail: 03 - Analysis, Finding 3 | 04 - Discoveries, #1

Finding 4: No Direct Imperative

The Quran never uses a direct "Fast!" command (صُمْ / صُومُوا). Instead, it uses indirect constructions: "fasting was prescribed for you" (كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ), "that you fast" (أَن تَصُومُوا), and "let him fast it" (فَلْيَصُمْهُ). The Quran also never narrates anyone actually fasting in the past tense (صَامَ). This suggests an invitation-based approach rather than a compulsion-based one.

Detail: 01 - Morphology, Observation 3 | 03 - Analysis, Finding 5

Finding 5: Ramadan Is About the Quran First

Verse 2:185 introduces Ramadan not through fasting but through the Quran's revelation: شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ. Fasting in Ramadan is presented as a response to this event. Traditional interpretations often invert this emphasis, presenting Ramadan primarily as the fasting month rather than the month of the Quran's revelation.
