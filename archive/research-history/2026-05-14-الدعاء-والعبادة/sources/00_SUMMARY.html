Root د ع و — Duʿāʾ and Its Placement Relative to Worship Commands

📑 Key Findings Summary

Finding 1: Direct Equation (المعادلة المباشرة)

The Quran explicitly equates duʿāʾ with ʿibādah in verse 40:60. The command to supplicate (ادعوني) is immediately followed by labeling its refusal as arrogance toward ʿibādah (عبادتي). This is the foundational proof that duʿāʾ is not a subcategory of worship — it is worship.

وَقَالَ رَبُّكُمُ ٱدْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْتَكْبِرُونَ عَنْ عِبَادَتِي سَيَدْخُلُونَ جَهَنَّمَ دَاخِرِينَ
غافر 40:60
"And your Lord said: Call upon Me; I will respond to you. Indeed, those who are too arrogant for My worship will enter Hell humiliated."

Finding 2: Proximity Pattern (نمط القرب النصي)

Duʿāʾ commands are consistently placed in immediate textual proximity to worship commands — after lordship declarations (7:54–56), within the fasting passage (2:186), alongside tawḥīd affirmations (40:65), and paired with prayer commands (20:14). This creates an inextricable contextual association.

Finding 3: Interchangeability (التبادلية)

The Quran uses the roots د ع و and ع ب د interchangeably when condemning shirk. The formula "I am forbidden to worship (أعبد) what you call upon (تدعون) besides Allah" (6:56, 40:66) treats duʿāʾ directed to other than Allah as identical to ʿibādah directed to other than Allah.

Conclusion (الخلاصة)

Duʿāʾ is the marrow of ʿibādah (مخّ العبادة). The Quran's deliberate placement strategies ensure this is understood not as an external association but as an intrinsic identity — to call upon Allah is to worship Him, and to call upon other than Allah is to worship other than Him.
