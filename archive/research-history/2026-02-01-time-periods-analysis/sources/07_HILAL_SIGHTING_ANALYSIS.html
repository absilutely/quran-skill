1. Quranic Foundation: الأهلة (The Crescents)

Verse 2:189 - The Purpose of Crescents
يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الْأَهِلَّةِ ۖ قُلْ هِيَ مَوَاقِيتُ لِلنَّاسِ وَالْحَجِّ
"They ask you about the crescents. Say: They are measurements of time for the people and for Hajj."

💡 Critical Linguistic Point

The word الأهلة (al-ahilla) is the plural of هِلال (hilal), which specifically means "crescent" — the visible new moon. This is NOT the astronomical new moon (conjunction), but the first sighting of the crescent after conjunction.

This means Islamic months are defined by visual observation, not astronomical calculation. This has profound implications for our time-period calculations.

2. Two Types of "Lunar Month"

Type Duration Definition Relevance
Synodic Month 29.53059 days Time between new moons (same phase) ✅ What humans COUNT (Islamic calendar)
Sidereal Month 27.32166 days Time for moon to orbit Earth once Actual orbital period
Difference 2.21 days ~8.1% longer for synodic Significant for calculations
Why Are They Different?

While the moon orbits Earth (27.32 days), Earth is also moving around the Sun.

For the moon to return to the same phase (new moon → new moon), it must travel an extra ~27° to "catch up" with Earth's new position relative to the Sun.

This extra distance takes ~2.21 additional days = synodic month (29.53 days)

3. Moon Distance: Perigee vs Apogee

The moon's orbit is elliptical, not circular. This means its distance from Earth varies significantly:

💡 Connection to Crescent Sighting

When the moon is at perigee (closer to Earth):

  • The crescent appears larger and brighter
  • It is easier to see with the naked eye
  • The month may be declared earlier

When the moon is at apogee (farther from Earth):
  • The crescent appears smaller and dimmer
  • It is harder to see
  • The month may be declared later

5. The Sighting-Distance Hypothesis

🔶 A Profound Possibility

What if the Quran's use of الأهلة (visible crescents) rather than calculated new moons is intentional?

Consider this chain of logic:

  1. The Quran uses الأهلة — visible crescents, not astronomical calculations
  2. Crescents are easier to see when the moon is closer (at or near perigee)
  3. Over thousands of sightings, there may be a statistical bias toward perigee-range distances
  4. If we calculate using perigee distance, we get ~103.8% of c — remarkably close!

💡 Statistical Consideration

While any individual new moon can occur at any point in the moon's elliptical orbit, the visibility of the crescent is better when the moon is closer. This could create a subtle bias in sighting-based calendars toward measuring time with a "closer-than-average" moon distance.

What Would Give Exactly c?

Reverse Engineering the "Correct" Distance:

Speed of light = 299,792 km/s Required distance in 1 day = 299,792 × 86,400 = 25,902,086,400 km Required distance per month = 25,902,086,400 ÷ 12,000 = 2,158,507 km Required radius = 2,158,507 ÷ (2π) = 343,605 km This is 12,895 km closer than perigee (356,500 km) Or about 3.6% closer than the closest approach This distance would occur during an unusually close perigee ("supermoon perigee")

Interestingly, the moon's closest possible approach (during a "supermoon" when perigee coincides with full/new moon) can be as close as ~356,400 km. The "perfect" distance for the calculation (343,605 km) is only about 3.6% closer than this.
