Traditional Scholarly Interpretations of Quranic Time Periods

Section 1: Traditional Tafsir Interpretations

Verse 22:47 (الحج)

وَإِنَّ يَوْمًا عِندَ رَبِّكَ كَأَلْفِ سَنَةٍ مِمَّا تَعُدُّونَ
"And indeed, a day with your Lord is like a thousand years of those which you count" (22:47)
Ibn Kathir (ابن كثير):

Emphasizes the divine perspective on time as fundamentally different from human perception. This verse establishes that from Allah's eternal viewpoint, the passage of a thousand human years is equivalent to a single day. Ibn Kathir interprets this as demonstrating Allah's omniscience and the illusory nature of temporal measurement when viewed from divine infinity. The focus is on the absolute difference between human and divine time perception.

Al-Qurtubi (القرطبي):

Discusses the relative nature of time and perception. Al-Qurtubi explores how time is subjective and depends on the consciousness experiencing it. For beings with different lifespans and perceptions (humans versus angels), a day may indeed encompass vastly different periods. This interpretation emphasizes the phenomenological nature of temporal experience rather than absolute measurement.

Al-Tabari (الطبري):

Presents multiple scholarly opinions on this verse without necessarily endorsing one interpretation. Al-Tabari catalogs various views: some scholars see it as purely metaphorical for divine eternity, others suggest it refers to a specific temporal ratio, and still others interpret it contextually within discussions of the Day of Judgment. This comprehensive approach acknowledges the verse's complexity.

Verse 32:5 (السجدة)

يُدَبِّرُ الْأَمْرَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ إِلَى الْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ فِي يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِمَّا تَعُدُّونَ
"He arranges [each] matter from the heaven to the earth; then it will ascend to Him in a Day, the extent of which is a thousand years of those which you count" (32:5)
Traditional View: Administrative Divine Order

Traditional tafsir scholars interpret this verse as describing Allah's management of affairs. The administrative order (الأمر) refers to all divine decrees and their execution. Commands descend from the heavens to the earth where they are implemented, and the results ascend back to Allah. This entire process takes "a day" equivalent to one thousand human years. The traditional understanding treats this as a comprehensive description of how the universe operates.

Discussion: What Does "الأمر" Refer To?

Scholars differ on the exact referent of الأمر (the matter/order/command):

  • Interpretation A: All divine decrees and their implementation in creation
  • Interpretation B: Specific commands related to human affairs and actions
  • Interpretation C: The management of celestial and terrestrial systems
  • Interpretation D: The cycle of divine judgment and reward

Verse 70:4 (المعارج)

تَعْرُجُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَالرُّوحُ إِلَيْهِ فِي يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُ خَمْسِينَ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ
"The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a day, the extent of which is fifty thousand years" (70:4)
Traditional View: Linked to Day of Judgment Duration

Classical tafsir scholars frequently link this verse to descriptions of the Day of Judgment. The fifty thousand year period is often interpreted as the duration of the resurrection day from the human perspective. Angels and spirits ascend to Allah during this extended period. The traditional interpretation emphasizes this as an eschatological description rather than a continuous cosmic process.

Different Scholarly Interpretations of the 50,000 Figure

Various scholars propose different explanations for why 70:4 specifies 50,000 years while 22:47 and 32:5 specify 1,000 years:

  • Contextual Difference: Different verses describe different processes (resurrection vs. divine management)
  • Pedagogical Reason: The Quran emphasizes the magnitude of the Day of Judgment through the larger figure
  • Temporal Scope: 50,000 years might represent the entire Day of Judgment while 1,000 years represents specific phases
  • Metaphorical Intensity: The increase signals the incomparability and difficulty of the Day of Judgment

Lorentz Factor: γ = t₀/t = 50,000/1 = 50

Time dilation formula: γ = 1/√(1 - v²/c²)

Solving for velocity:
50 = 1/√(1 - v²/c²)
√(1 - v²/c²) = 1/50 = 0.02
