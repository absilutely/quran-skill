Category 1: Cosmic Day = 1000 Years

▶

Two verses that establish the equivalence of one cosmic day to one thousand years in human time.

Surah As-Sajdah (32), Verse 5 32:5
يُدَبِّرُ ٱلْأَمْرَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ فِى يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُۥٓ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ
Translation: "He arranges [each] matter from the heaven to the earth; then it will ascend to Him in a Day, the extent of which is a thousand years of those which you count."
Key Analysis: This verse explicitly states that from Allah's divine perspective, one day of cosmic management and judgment equals 1,000 human years. The term "يَدَبِّرُ" (arranges/manages) indicates divine oversight of all affairs, while "يَعْرُجُ" (ascends) connects to the theme of spiritual ascension. The phrase "مِمَّا تَعُدُّونَ" (of those you count) emphasizes the relativity of time measurement between the divine and human domains.
Time Equivalence 1000 Years يدبر يعرج
Cross-References: See 22:47 for parallel statement; 70:4 for alternative cosmic period (50,000 years)
Surah Al-Hajj (22), Verse 47 22:47
وَيَسْتَعْجِلُونَكَ بِٱلْعَذَابِ وَلَن يُخْلِفَ ٱللَّهُ وَعْدَهُۥ ۚ وَإِنَّ يَوْمًا عِندَ رَبِّكَ كَأَلْفِ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ
Translation: "And they urge you to hasten the punishment. But Allah will never fail in His promise. And indeed, a day with your Lord is like a thousand years of those which you count."
Key Analysis: This verse addresses those who questioned the promise of divine judgment, presenting the cosmic time-scale to explain why the Day of Judgment seems delayed from a human perspective. The comparison "كَأَلْفِ سَنَةٍ" (like a thousand years) emphasizes that divine time operates on an entirely different scale. The context stresses Allah's faithfulness to His promise despite the perception of delay in human terms.
Judgment Day Time Perception Divine Promise 1000 Years
Cross-References: See 32:5 for parallel statement about cosmic time; Compare with 70:4 for the 50,000-year period

Category 2: Cosmic Day = 50,000 Years

Translation: "The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him during a Day the extent of which is fifty thousand years."
Key Analysis: This verse presents a different cosmic time scale (50,000 years vs. 1,000 years) within the context of eschatological events. The use of "تَعْرُجُ" (ascend) connects to the root ع-ر-ج, emphasizing spiritual ascension. The mention of "الملائكة والروح" (angels and the Spirit) suggests this refers specifically to the Day of Judgment when all creation returns to the divine. This verse appears in Surah Al-Ma'arij (The Paths of Ascent), reinforcing the theme of ascension and divine time scales.
50,000 Years Judgment Day Angels Ascend تعرج

Category 3: سنة vs عام Distinction

▶

Critical verse showing the semantic distinction between سنة (year as a cycle/season) and عام (year as a unit of time). This verse demonstrates that the Quran uses these terms with different meanings.

Surah Al-Ankabut (29), Verse 14 29:14
وَلَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا نُوحًا إِلَىٰ قَوْمِهِۦ فَلَبِثَ فِيهِمْ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمْسِينَ عَامًا
Translation: "And We certainly sent Noah to his people, and he remained among them a thousand years (سنة) minus fifty years (عام)."
Key Analysis: This is a crucial verse for understanding Quranic terminology. Noah preached for "ألف سنة" (a thousand سنة/cycles) minus "خمسين عام" (fifty عام/years). The deliberate use of two different words in the same context suggests they carry distinct meanings:

• سنة (Sunnah/Cycle): Refers to established patterns, cycles, or extended periods. In this context, likely referring to generational cycles or divinely ordained patterns.

• عام (Aam/Year): Refers to a specific unit of time (calendar year), often used for precise temporal measurements.

This semantic distinction is fundamental to interpreting the cosmic time periods in 32:5, 22:47, and 70:4. The use of سنة in those verses may carry connotations beyond literal solar years.
Semantic Analysis سنة عام Noah
Cross-References: See Category 4 for other uses of عام; Compare linguistic usage with verses in Category 1 that use سنة for cosmic periods

Category 4: عام Only Verses

▶

Verses using only عام (calendar year) for temporal references, demonstrating its use for precise, measurable time periods.

Surah Al-Baqarah (2), Verse 259 2:259
أَوْ كَٱلَّذِى مَرَّ عَلَىٰ قَرْيَةٍ وَهِىَ خَاوِيَةٌ عَلَىٰ عُرُوشِهَا قَالَ أَنَّىٰ يُحْىِۦ هَـٰذِهِ ٱللَّهُ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا ۖ فَأَمَاتَهُ ٱللَّهُ مِائَةَ عَامٍ ثُمَّ بَعَثَهُ
Translation: "Or [consider the example of] one who passed by a settlement that had fallen into ruin. He said, 'How will Allah restore this to life after its death?' So Allah caused him to die for a hundred years, then brought him back to life."
Key Analysis: This verse uses عام (calendar year) explicitly for a precise temporal measurement: "مائة عام" (a hundred years). The context describes a miraculous suspension of life for exactly 100 years before resurrection. The use of عام here emphasizes a specific, measurable duration of time. This contrasts with the use of سنة in cosmic contexts, supporting the argument that عام denotes concrete, calculable time periods.
100 Years عام Resurrection Death and Life
Cross-References: See 29:14 for comparison of سنة and عام; Related to eschatological themes in Category 1
Surah At-Tawbah (9), Verse 126 9:126
أَوَلَا يَرَوْنَ أَنَّهُمْ يُفْتَنُونَ فِى كُلِّ عَامٍ مَّرَّةً أَوْ مَرَّتَيْنِ
Translation: "Do they not see that they are tested every year once or twice?"
Key Analysis: This verse uses عام in a cyclical context ("كل عام") to describe annual testing. The phrase "في كل عام مرة أو مرتين" (every year one or two times) demonstrates عام as a recurring, measurable time unit. This usage reinforces that عام refers to specific calendar years used for recurring events, contrasting with the metaphysical connotations possible with سنة in cosmic contexts.
عام Annual Testing Trial
Cross-References: Compare usage of عام with 29:14 and 2:259
Surah Yusuf (12), Verse 49 12:49
ثُمَّ يَأْتِى مِن بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ عَامٌ فِيهِ يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ وَفِيهِ يَعْصِرُونَ
Translation: "Then will come after that a year in which the people will be given relief and in which they will press [olives and grapes for oil and wine]."
Key Analysis: This verse uses عام to describe a specific year of agricultural abundance following seven years of famine. The phrase "عام فيه يغاث الناس" (a year in which people are given relief) demonstrates عام as a concrete, bounded time period with specific events. The agricultural context emphasizes that عام refers to actual calendar years with observable seasonal and economic changes, reinforcing its distinction from the metaphorical or cyclical connotations of سنة.
عام Year of Relief Famine Agriculture
Cross-References: See 2:259 and 9:126 for other uses of عام

Category 5: Astronomical Calculation (حسبان)

▶

Verses emphasizing the astronomical basis for calculating years and understanding cosmic time through the movements of celestial bodies.

Surah Ar-Rahman (55), Verse 5 55:5
ٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ بِحُسْبَانٍ
Translation: "The sun and the moon [move] by precise calculation."
Key Analysis: This concise but profound verse establishes that the sun and moon operate "بِحُسْبَانٍ" (by precise calculation/computation). The term "حسبان" encompasses mathematical precision, astronomical regularity, and divine determination. This verse is foundational to understanding how years are measured: through the regular, calculable motions of celestial bodies. The mathematical precision of solar and lunar movements enables the definition of years and the distinction between different time-measuring systems.
Astronomical Calculation حسبان Sun and Moon Precision
Cross-References: See 10:5 and 6:96 for detailed descriptions of lunar and solar functions in time reckoning
Surah Yunus (10), Verse 5 10:5
هُوَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ ٱلشَّمْسَ ضِيَآءً وَٱلْقَمَرَ نُورًا وَقَدَّرَهُۥ مَنَازِلَ لِتَعْلَمُوا عَدَدَ ٱلسِّنِينَ وَٱلْحِسَابَ ۚ مَا خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ذَٰلِكَ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ ۚ يُفَصِّلُ ٱلْآيَـٰتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ
Translation: "It is He who made the sun a shining light and the moon a reflected light and determined for it phases - that you may know the number of years and [the] calculation [of time]. Allah has not created this except in truth. He details the verses for a people who know."
Key Analysis: This comprehensive verse explains the purpose of lunar phases: to enable humans to "تعلموا عدد السنين والحساب" (know the number of years and calculation). The moon's 28-day cycle creates the monthly and yearly calendar systems. Significantly, this verse uses both "السنين" (years) and "الحساب" (calculation/reckoning). The divine purpose in creating these celestial motions is explicitly stated as providing the means for temporal calculation. This verse directly connects astronomical observation to the definition of years and time measurement systems.
Moon Phases Year Calculation عدد السنين الحساب
Cross-References: See 55:5 and 6:96 for related astronomical concepts; Compare with 29:14 for the use of السنين
Surah Al-An'am (6), Verse 96 6:96

Translation: "[He is] the cleaver of the daybreak and has made the night for rest and the sun and moon for reckoning. That is the determination of the Exalted in Might, the All-Knowing."
Key Analysis: This verse describes the sun and moon as "حسبان" (reckoning/calculation), emphasizing their role in temporal measurement. The phrase "وَجَعَلَ ٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ حُسْبَانًا" makes explicit that these celestial bodies exist for the purpose of time reckoning. Combined with verses 55:5 and 10:5, this establishes that the astronomical systems are the foundation for all temporal measurement in the Quranic worldview. The description "ذَٰلِكَ تَقْدِيرُ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْعَلِيمِ" (That is the determination of the Exalted in Might, the All-Knowing) emphasizes the divine precision of these systems.
Sun and Moon Reckoning حسبان Day and Night
Cross-References: See 55:5 and 10:5 for comprehensive discussion of astronomical calculation
Surah Ibrahim (14), Verse 33 14:33
وَسَخَّرَ لَكُمُ ٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ دَآئِبَيْنِ ۖ وَسَخَّرَ لَكُمُ ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ
Translation: "And He has subjected to you the sun and the moon, both constantly pursuing their courses; and He has subjected to you the night and the day."
Key Analysis: This verse emphasizes that the sun and moon move continuously ("دآئبين") in their respective orbits, creating the foundation for measuring time through day-night cycles and seasons. The word "دآئبين" (constantly pursuing) indicates their reliable, predictable nature - the basis for astronomical calculation and temporal measurement.
Constant Motion دآئبين Time Measurement

Surah Luqman (31), Verse 29 31:29
أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُولِجُ ٱلَّيْلَ فِى ٱلنَّهَارِ وَيُولِجُ ٱلنَّهَارَ فِى ٱلَّيْلِ وَسَخَّرَ ٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ كُلٌّ يَجْرِى إِلَىٰ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى

Both the sun and moon are bound by predetermined orbital periods. This divine ordering of celestial mechanics is the foundation for all time measurement systems. The recognition of these appointed terms allows humans to calculate years, seasons, and cosmic time scales.
Appointed Term أجل مسمى Orbital Mechanics

Category 6: Ascension Root ع ر ج (Ascending/Paths)

▶

Verses using the root ع-ر-ج (ascend/ascension) which appears frequently in verses discussing cosmic time and divine ascent. This root suggests a connection between temporal ascension and spiritual ascension.

Surah As-Sajdah (32), Verse 5 32:5
يُدَبِّرُ ٱلْأَمْرَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ ثُمَّ يَعْرُجُ إِلَيْهِ فِى يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُۥٓ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ
Translation: "He arranges [each] matter from the heaven to the earth; then it will ascend to Him in a Day, the extent of which is a thousand years of those which you count."
Key Analysis: The verb "يَعْرُجُ" (ascends) uses the ع-ر-ج root, describing the upward movement of affairs from earth to heaven. This ascension occurs within "a day" that measures a thousand years, connecting spiritual/temporal ascension with cosmic time.
يعرج Ascend ع ر ج
Surah Saba (34), Verse 2 34:2
يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنزِلُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلرَّحِيمُ ٱلْغَفُورُ
Translation: "He knows what penetrates into the earth and what comes out of it and what descends from the heaven and what ascends therein. And He is the Most Merciful, the Most Forgiving."
Key Analysis: This verse uses "يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا" (ascends in it), referring to what ascends into the heavens. The ع-ر-ج root appears in the context of divine omniscience regarding vertical movement and spiritual ascent. Allah's knowledge encompasses all ascending and descending movements, whether physical or spiritual.
يعرج Heavenly Ascent ع ر ج
Cross-References: See 70:4 for "تَعْرُجُ" (angels ascend); Compare with 32:5
Surah Al-Hadid (57), Verse 4 57:4
هُوَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ فِى سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰ عَلَى ٱلْعَرْشِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنزِلُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا ۚ وَهُوَ مَعَكُمْ أَيْنَ مَا كُنتُمْ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ
Translation: "It is He who created the heavens and the earth in six days and then established Himself above the Throne. Notably, this verse also mentions creation in "سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ" (six days), connecting earthly creation time with cosmic time concepts. The ascending motion ("يَعْرُجُ") represents movement toward the divine realm, complementing the descending motion ("يَنزِلُ").
يعرج Creation in Six Days ع ر ج
Cross-References: See 34:2 for parallel usage; Compare "six days" with 70:3 context

Category 7: Related Time Concepts & Divine Regulation

▶

Additional verses discussing time concepts, divine regulation of temporal affairs, and eschatological timing.

Surah Al-Mu'minun (23), Verses 12-14 23:12-14
وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَـٰنَ مِن سُلَـٰلَةٍ مِّن طِينٍ (12) ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَـٰهُ نُطْفَةً فِى قَرَارٍ مَّكِينٍ (13) ثُمَّ خَلَقْنَا ٱلنُّطْفَةَ عَلَقَةً فَخَلَقْنَا ٱلْعَلَقَةَ مُضْغَةً فَخَلَقْنَا ٱلْمُضْغَةَ عِظَـٰمًا فَكَسَوْنَا ٱلْعِظَـٰمَ لَحْمًا ثُمَّ أَنشَأْنَـٰهُ خَلْقًا ءَاخَرَ ۚ فَتَبَارَكَ ٱللَّهُ أَحْسَنُ ٱلْخَـٰلِقِينَ (14)
Translation: "And We certainly created man from an extract of clay. (12) Then We placed him as a sperm-drop in a place of settlement, firmly fixed. So blessed is Allah, the best of creators. (14)"
Key Analysis: These verses describe stages of human development, each with temporal progression: clay → sperm-drop → clinging form → lump → bones → flesh → complete human. The sequential "ثُمَّ" (then) indicates temporal progression. This demonstrates how the Quran discusses time in stages and cycles at different scales - from embryonic development to cosmic ages. The structured, stage-by-stage development reflects the same principle of time measurement found in larger cosmic time scales.
Creation Stages Temporal Progression Developmental Time
Surah Fussilat (41), Verses 9-12 41:9-12
قُلْ أَئِنَّكُمْ لَتَكْفُرُونَ بِٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ٱلْأَرْضَ فِى يَوْمَيْنِ وَتَجْعَلُونَ لَهُۥٓ أَندَادًا ۚ ذَٰلِكَ رَبُّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ (9) وَجَعَلَ فِيهَا رَوَٰسِىَ مِن فَوْقِهَا وَبَـٰرَكَ فِيهَا وَقَدَّرَ فِيهَآ أَقْوَٰتَهَا فِى أَرْبَعَةِ أَيَّامٍ سَوَآءً لِّلسَّآئِلِينَ (10) ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَهِىَ دُخَانٌ فَقَالَ لَهَا وَلِلْأَرْضِ ٱئْتِيَا طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًا قَالَتَآ أَتَيْنَا طَآئِعِينَ (11) فَقَضَىٰهُنَّ سَبْعَ سَمَـٰوَٰتٍ فِى يَوْمَيْنِ وَأَوْحَىٰ فِى كُلِّ سَمَآءٍ أَمْرَهَا ۚ وَزَيَّنَّا ٱلسَّمَآءَ ٱلدُّنْيَا بِمِصْبَـٰحٍ ۚ وَذَٰلِكَ تَقْدِيرُ ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلْعَلِيمِ (12)
Translation: "Say, 'Do you indeed disbelieve in the One who created the earth in two days and attribute to Him equals? That is the Lord of the worlds. (9) And He placed within the earth firmly set mountains over its surface, and He blessed it and determined therein its [creatures'] sustenance in four days without distinction - for [the information of] those who ask. (10) Then He directed Himself to the heaven while it was smoke and said to it and to the earth, 'Come [both of you] willingly or by compulsion.' They said, 'We have come willingly.' (11) And He completed them as seven heavens within two days and inspired in each heaven its command. And We adorned the nearest heaven with lamps and as protection.
