2. Classical Tafsir Interpretations

Ibn Kathir (Abridged, English — ID 169)

Interpretation: "We made it vast and We brought its roof higher without pillars to support it, and thus it is hanging independently."

Key Points:

  • Understands لَمُوسِعُونَ as "We made it vast" — widening of the sky's extent
  • Cites Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Thawri for بِأَيْدٍ meaning "with strength"
  • Focuses on the sky being raised as a roof without pillars

Assessment: Partial — Captures the vastness aspect but treats it as completed (past tense) rather than ongoing. Does not address the continuous nature of the active participle.

Al-Qurtubi (Arabic — ID 90)

Key interpretations cited:

  • Ibn Abbas (1st view): لَقادرون — "We are capable/powerful" (understanding موسع as having ability/means)
  • Ibn Abbas (2nd view): لَمُوسِعُونَ الرزق على خلقنا — "We expand provision for Our creation"
  • Ad-Dahhak: أغنياء — "We are wealthy/self-sufficient" (citing 2:236 — على الموسع قدره)
  • Al-Hasan: مطيقون — "We are capable"
  • Al-Jawhari: أوسع الرجل = صار ذا سعة وغنى — "the man became wealthy." So the meaning is "We are wealthy/capable."

Al-Qurtubi's conclusion: The meanings are all close to each other (والمعنى متقارب). Some also say: "We made between it and the earth a vastness."

Assessment: Partial — Presents multiple valid linguistic interpretations. The interpretation of "expanding provision" is closest to the physical expansion reading. However, none explicitly address ongoing cosmic expansion.

Al-Tabari (Arabic — ID 15)

Interpretation: لذو سعة بخلقها وخلق ما شئنا أن نخلقه وقدرة عليه — "We are possessors of vastness/ability in creating it and creating whatever We wish, and power over it."

Key detail: Al-Tabari cites Ibn Zayd who simply said: أوسعها جلّ جلاله — "He expanded it, Glorious is He."

Assessment: Partial — The Ibn Zayd citation is notable: أوسعها directly means "He expanded it" — the pronoun هَا refers to the heaven. This is the closest classical interpretation to the cosmic expansion meaning.

Al-Sa'di (Arabic — ID 91)

Interpretation: لَموسعون لأرجائها وأنحائها، وإنا لموسعون أيضا على عبادنا بالرزق — "We are expanders of its regions and directions, and We are also expanders upon Our servants with provision."

Assessment: High — Al-Sa'di captures both meanings: (1) physical expansion of the sky's regions, and (2) expansion of provision. His primary meaning (لأرجائها وأنحائها — "its regions and directions") is strikingly close to cosmic expansion.

Ibn Ashur (At-Tahrir wat-Tanwir — from Arabic text retrieved)

Interpretation: المُوسِع: اسم فاعل من أوسع، إذا كان ذا وُسع، أي قدرة — "Al-Mūsi': active participle from awsa'a, meaning to be one who possesses capacity/ability."

He notes the word's derivations come from سَعة (spaciousness) — extension of physical space, abundance of resources, and comprehensiveness. He connects it to the divine name الواسع.

Assessment: Medium — Thorough linguistic analysis but focuses more on the ability/capacity interpretation than physical expansion.

5. Historical Note

It is significant that some classical scholars did understand لَمُوسِعُونَ as physical expansion of the sky, even without knowledge of modern cosmology:

  • Ibn Zayd (as cited by Al-Tabari): أوسعها — "He expanded it" (the heaven)
  • Al-Sa'di: لَموسعون لأرجائها وأنحائها — "expanders of its regions and directions"
  • Some scholars: "We made between it and the earth a vastness" (i.e., expanded the space between sky and earth)

The physical expansion interpretation was not invented by modern commentators — it has roots in classical tafsir tradition, though it was not the majority view.
