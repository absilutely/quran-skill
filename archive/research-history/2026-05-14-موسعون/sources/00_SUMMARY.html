2. Key Findings (Quran-Derived)

Finding 1: وَسِعَ (wasiʿa) — Divine Encompassing

The verb وَسِعَ in Form I appears 7 times, always describing Allah's attributes encompassing all things. The consistent pattern is "وَسِعَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ عِلْمًا" (His knowledge encompasses everything) — found in 6:80, 7:89, 20:98, and "وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ" (His Seat/Kursi encompasses the heavens and earth) in 2:255.

Evidence: 20:98, 6:80, 7:89, 2:255

Finding 2: وَاسِع (Wāsiʿ) — A Divine Name

The active participle وَاسِع appears 8 times in the Quran, exclusively as a divine attribute. It is always paired with عَلِيم (All-Knowing) — forming the pair وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ. This reveals that in the Quran, divine "vastness" is inseparable from divine "knowledge." God's capacity/encompassing is always linked to His awareness.

Evidence: 2:115, 2:261, 2:268, 3:73, 5:54, 24:32, 53:32

Finding 3: وُسْعَهَا (wusʿahā) — Capacity/Burden

The noun وُسْع (capacity) with possessive suffixes appears in the context of divine justice: Allah does not burden a soul beyond its وُسْع (capacity). This reveals that وُسْع means not just "width" but "the extent of what something can contain/bear."

Evidence: 2:286, 7:42, 23:62, 6:152, 2:233

Finding 4: وَاسِعَة (wāsiʿa) — Physical/Spatial Vastness

The feminine form وَاسِعَة describes physical vastness: God's mercy (رَحْمَة وَاسِعَة in 6:147, 7:156), God's earth (أَرْضِي وَاسِعَة in 29:56), and the earth being wide for migration (4:97). This form applies to both abstract (mercy) and physical (earth) vastness.

Evidence: 6:147, 7:156, 29:56, 4:97

Finding 5: لَمُوسِعُونَ (lamūsiʿūn) — The Expanding Universe (51:47)

The most remarkable occurrence of this root is in 51:47:

وَالسَّمَاءَ بَنَيْنَاهَا بِأَيْدٍ وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ

"And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander." (Sahih International)

"We built the heavens with Our power and made them vast." (Abdel Haleem)

Morphological analysis: لَمُوسِعُونَ is the active participle (اسم فاعل) of Form IV (أَوْسَعَ), in masculine plural form with the emphatic لَ prefix. Form IV (أَفْعَلَ) means "to cause to be wide / to expand / to make vast." The active participle indicates an ongoing state — "We are [continuously] expanding."

Science connection: This is remarkably consistent with the discovery of the expanding universe by Edwin Hubble in 1929, later confirmed by modern cosmology. The use of the active participle (ongoing state) rather than a past tense verb aligns precisely with the continuous nature of cosmic expansion. 3. Traditional vs. Quranic Comparison

AspectTraditional UnderstandingQuranic Analysis
وَاسِع as divine name Generally translated as "All-Encompassing" or "Vast" Always paired with عَلِيم — the Quran itself links vastness to knowledge, suggesting God's encompassing is primarily through knowledge and capacity, not just physical size
لَمُوسِعُونَ in 51:47 Traditional tafsirs: "We made it vast" or "We are wealthy/capable" or "We provide abundantly" (Ibn Abbas, Al-Qurtubi) The active participle form indicates ongoing action. Combined with the context of بَنَيْنَاهَا (We built it), the verse describes both the initial construction AND ongoing expansion of the heaven — a meaning that aligns with modern cosmology
وُسْع (capacity) Simply "ability" or "capacity" The Quran uses it specifically for the maximum extent something can bear — a precise measure, not a vague "ability." This is why it's used with لَا يُكَلِّفُ (does not burden) — it's about exact limits
سَعَة in 65:7 "Wealth" or "means" The Quran contrasts سَعَة with قُدِرَ عَلَيْهِ رِزْقُهُ (restricted provision), revealing that سَعَة means abundance/expansion of resources — the opposite of restriction
