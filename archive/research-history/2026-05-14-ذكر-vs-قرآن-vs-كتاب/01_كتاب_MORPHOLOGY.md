# Morphological Analysis: كِتَاب (Kitab)

## Root Letters
**ك ت ب** (kaf-ta-ba)

## Lexical Meaning (من لسان العرب والمفردات)
The root ك ت ب means:
1. **To write** (الكتابة): The physical act of inscribing
2. **To prescribe/decree** (الفرض): To make obligatory, to ordain
3. **To gather/join** (الجمع): To bring together, collect

Al-Raghib al-Isfahani notes that كِتَاب implies something that is **fixed, recorded, and permanent**. It carries connotations of authority, prescription, and official documentation.

## Morphological Pattern

### كِتَاب - Pattern: فِعَال
| Aspect | Analysis |
|--------|----------|
| Pattern | فِعَال (fi'āl) |
| Type | Noun of instrument (اسم آلة) or intensive noun |
| Root meaning | A written document, scripture, decree |
| Plural | كُتُب (kutub) |

The pattern فِعَال can denote:
- The instrument or means of action
- The product of an action
- Intensity or profession

## Key Observations

1. **Written/Fixed Nature**: Unlike قُرْآن (which emphasizes recitation), كِتَاب emphasizes something **written, recorded, and authoritative**.

2. **Universal Category**: كِتَاب is a broader term that applies to:
   - The Quran
   - Torah
   - Injeel
   - Zabur
   - Records of deeds
   - Divine decree (اللوح المحفوظ)

3. **Continuity with Previous Revelations**: The heavy use of أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ shows that كِتَاب connects the Quran to a **continuum of divine scriptures**.

4. **Prescriptive Authority**: The root meaning of "to prescribe" appears in usages like كِتَابًا مَّوْقُوتًا (4:103), where prayer is a "prescribed" obligation.

5. **Textual Permanence**: Phrases like أُمُّ الْكِتَابِ (Mother of the Book) and الْكِتَابُ الْمُبِينُ (Clear Book) suggest a heavenly archetype from which earthly scriptures derive.

6. **Relationship to قُرْآن**: In 41:3, the revelation is called a كِتَاب whose verses were made detailed "as قُرْآنًا عَرَبِيًّا" - suggesting كتاب is the **entity** while قرآن describes its **mode/form**.
