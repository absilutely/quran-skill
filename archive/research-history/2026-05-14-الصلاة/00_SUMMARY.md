# Research Summary: الصلاة / الصلوة

## Research Question
What is the meaning of الصلاة in the Quran, considering its various written forms (with and without و)?

## Methodology
- Quran-only analysis (traditional sources used only for comparison)
- Exhaustive verse collection from root ص ل و / ص ل ي
- Morphological analysis of all variations
- Contextual pattern analysis
- Derivation of meaning from Quranic usage alone

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Two Distinct Semantic Fields

The research revealed that the Quran uses two **different roots** that are sometimes confused:

| Root | Verb Form | Meaning | Context |
|------|-----------|---------|---------|
| ص ل و | صَلَّى (Form II) | To connect, bless, pray | Positive - worship, mercy |
| ص ل ي | صَلِيَ (Form I) | To burn, enter fire | Negative - punishment |

**Evidence:**
- يُصَلِّى عَلَيْكُمْ (33:43) - He blesses you
- يَصْلَى ٱلنَّارَ (87:12) - He burns in fire

### 2. الصلاة is "Established" Not "Performed"

**Critical Discovery:** The Quran **exclusively** uses أَقَامَ (to establish) with الصلاة, never أَدَّى (to perform).

**Implication:** الصلاة is a **state to be maintained**, not a ritual to be completed and finished.

**Evidence:** Over 35 instances of أَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ, يُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ, etc.

### 3. الصلاة Prevents Evil

> إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ تَنْهَىٰ عَنِ ٱلْفَحْشَآءِ وَٱلْمُنكَرِ (29:45)

The Quran explicitly states that الصلاة has a **preventative function** against immorality and wrongdoing.

### 4. Allah's صلاة = Blessing

When Allah يُصَلِّى upon believers (33:43), it means He blesses them and brings them from darkness to light. This reveals the core meaning of the root: **connection and support**.

### 5. Empty صلاة is Condemned

- The disbelievers' صلاة was "whistling and clapping" (8:35)
- Those who pray while "heedless" are warned (107:4-5)
- Presence (خشوع) is essential (23:2)

## Traditional vs. Quranic Interpretation

| Aspect | Traditional Understanding | Quranic Evidence Shows |
|--------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| **Definition** | The five daily ritual prayers with specific movements | A state of connection to Allah that includes but transcends ritual |
| **Verb used** | Often "أَدَّى الصلاة" (performed prayer) | Exclusively "أَقَامَ الصلاة" (established prayer) |
| **Scope** | Limited to specific times and rituals | Encompasses all of life (6:162) |
| **Allah's صلاة** | Usually translated as "blessings" | Confirms: connection, mercy, support |
| **Validity** | Valid if physical requirements met | Requires خشوع (humility) and دوام (consistency) |

---

## The و Spelling (الصلوة)

The Uthmani Mushaf consistently uses الصلوة (with و) rather than الصلاة (with ا). This ancient spelling may connect to صِلة (connection/relationship), emphasizing the relational nature of prayer.

---

## Quran-Derived Definition

**الصلاة is:**

> A continuous state of connection (صِلة) between the servant and Allah, established (أُقيمَت) through conscious devotion, manifesting as:
> - Worship and supplication from the human to Allah
> - Blessing and mercy from Allah to the human
>
> When properly established, it prevents evil and encompasses all aspects of life.

## Key Insights on Divine Word Choice

1. **Why أَقَامَ not أَدَّى?** (See detailed analysis below)
   - أَقَامَ implies permanence, maintenance, uprightness
   - الصلاة is not something you "do and finish" but something you "establish and maintain"

2. **Why paired with الزكاة?**
   - الصلاة = internal connection (established)
   - الزكاة = external giving (given)
   - Together they represent complete devotion: inward and outward

3. **Why does Allah يُصَلِّى?**
   - Reveals the core meaning: connection, support, blessing
   - Human صلاة is reaching up; Divine صلاة is reaching down
   - The same root describes both because both are acts of connection

4. **Why الصلوة with و?**
   - May preserve linguistic connection to صِلة (relationship)
   - Emphasizes the relational nature of worship

## Detailed Analysis: أَقَامَ vs أَدَّى

### The Quranic Choice: Why أَقَامَ for الصلاة?

A critical observation in Quranic linguistics is that Allah **exclusively** uses the verb أَقَامَ (to establish/maintain) with الصلاة, and **never** uses أَدَّى (to perform/fulfill). This is not accidental—it reveals the essential nature of الصلاة.

### Root Analysis

| Verb | Root | Pattern | Core Meaning |
|------|------|---------|--------------|
| أَقَامَ | ق و م | أَفْعَلَ (Form IV) | To make stand, establish, maintain upright |
| أَدَّى | أ د ي | فَعَّلَ (Form II) | To deliver, fulfill, discharge, complete |

### How أَدَّى is Used in the Quran

The verb أَدَّى appears in the Quran for **transactional obligations**—things that are delivered, handed over, or completed:

| Verse | Arabic Text | Object | Meaning |
|-------|------------|--------|---------|
| 4:58 | تُؤَدُّوا۟ ٱلْأَمَـٰنَـٰتِ | الأمانات (trusts) | Deliver trusts to their owners |
| 2:283 | فَلْيُؤَدِّ ٱلَّذِى ٱؤْتُمِنَ أَمَـٰنَتَهُۥ | الأمانة (trust) | Let the trusted one deliver the trust |
| 44:18 | أَدُّوٓا۟ إِلَىَّ عِبَادَ ٱللَّهِ | عباد الله (servants) | Deliver to me the servants of Allah |

**Pattern:** أَدَّى is used for things that are:
- **Transferred** from one party to another
- **Completed** and finished
- **Discharged** as an obligation
- **Handed over** as a transaction

### How أَقَامَ is Used with الصلاة

| Verse | Arabic Text | Translation |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| 2:43 | وَأَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ | And establish the prayer |
| 2:110 | وَأَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتُوا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ | Establish prayer and give zakah |
| 14:40 | رَبِّ ٱجْعَلْنِى مُقِيمَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ | My Lord, make me an establisher of prayer |
| 29:45 | وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ | And establish the prayer |

**Pattern:** أَقَامَ implies:
- **Permanence** - something that stands continuously
- **Uprightness** - correctness and straightness
- **Maintenance** - ongoing care and attention
- **Foundation** - not a transaction but a state of being

### The Semantic Contrast

| Aspect | أَدَّى الصلاة (NOT used) | أَقَامَ الصلاة (USED) |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| **Nature** | Transaction | State |
| **Duration** | Finite, ends when done | Continuous, maintained |
| **Focus** | Completing an act | Maintaining a connection |
| **Result** | Obligation discharged | Relationship sustained |
| **Implication** | Ritual performed | Life transformed |

### Why This Matters

If Allah had used "أَدُّوا الصلاة" (perform/deliver the prayer), it would imply:
- Prayer is a task to complete and be done with
- Prayer is a transaction between you and Allah
- Once performed, the obligation is discharged
- You can move on after "delivering" it

But by using "أَقِيمُوا الصلاة" (establish the prayer), Allah indicates:
- Prayer is a state to maintain, not a task to complete
- Prayer is a continuous connection, not a transaction
- The obligation is never "discharged"—it's a perpetual establishment
- Prayer transforms your entire life, not just moments within it

### Supporting Evidence: The دَائِمُونَ Connection

This understanding is reinforced by 70:23:

> ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَلَىٰ صَلَاتِهِمْ دَآئِمُونَ
> "Those who are **constant** (دَائِمُونَ) upon their prayer"

The word دَائِم (constant, permanent) echoes the meaning of أَقَامَ—both indicate something that stands permanently, not something performed and finished.

### Contrast with الزكاة

Interestingly, while الصلاة uses أَقَامَ (establish), الزكاة uses آتَى (to give):

> وَأَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتُوا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ (2:43)
> "Establish the prayer and **give** the zakah"

This reveals:
- **الصلاة** is internal and continuous—it must be **established**
- **الزكاة** is external and transactional—it is **given**

Both are essential, but they operate differently. Prayer is a permanent state; zakah is a periodic act.

### Conclusion

The Quran's exclusive use of أَقَامَ with الصلاة is a precise linguistic choice that reveals the true nature of prayer:

**الصلاة is not a ritual you perform and complete.**
**الصلاة is a connection you establish and maintain.**

This distinction has profound implications for how we understand and practice prayer—not as moments of worship separated from life, but as a continuous state of connection to Allah that encompasses all of existence:

> قُلْ إِنَّ صَلَاتِى وَنُسُكِى وَمَحْيَاىَ وَمَمَاتِى لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ (6:162)
> "Say: Indeed, my prayer, my sacrifice, my living, and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds."
