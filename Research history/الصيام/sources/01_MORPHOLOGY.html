01 - Morphology: الصِّيَام

Root: ص و م (ṣ-w-m)

Lexical meaning (Lisan al-Arab): To abstain, refrain, cease, hold back from something. The root carries the core meaning of imsāk (إمساك) — withholding or restraining oneself.

Al-Raghib al-Isfahani (Al-Mufradat): الصوم: الإمساك عن الفعل مطعماً كان أو كلاماً أو مشياً
"Al-Ṣawm: Refraining from an action — whether it be eating, speaking, or walking."

Key Morphological Observations

1. Two Distinct Masdar Forms

The Quran uses two different masdar forms from the same root:

  • صِيَام (فِعَال pattern) — appears in all food-fasting contexts (ritual fasting, Ramadan, expiation)
  • صَوْم (فَعْل pattern) — appears only once in 19:26, meaning abstention from speech

This is a critical distinction: the Quran appears to consistently use صِيَام for the religious act of fasting from food/drink, while صَوْم is used for a broader, more general abstention (from speech in the Maryam context).

2. Only Form I Used

No higher verb forms (II–X) appear in the Quran for this root. Fasting in the Quran is always presented as a personal, voluntary act (Form I) — never as something imposed on others (Form II/IV causative), nor as a reflexive or passive action.

3. Limited Verb Conjugations

Only two verb forms appear: تَصُومُوا (you all fast) and فَلْيَصُمْهُ (let him fast it). Notable absences include the past tense (صَامَ) and the direct imperative (صُمْ). The Quran never narrates "they fasted" or commands "fast!" directly — instead using indirect constructions.

4. Active Participle Usage

The active participles الصَّائِمِينَ and الصَّائِمَاتِ appear only in 33:35, in a list describing persistent character traits. The فَاعِل pattern indicates a habitual quality — not a one-time act but an ongoing characteristic of the person.
