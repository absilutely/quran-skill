Critical Translation Assessment

Key Issue: صَوْم vs. صِيَام Distinction

Sahih International correctly distinguishes the two by translating صَوْمًا as "abstention" (19:26) and صِيَام as "fasting" elsewhere. This preserves the Quranic distinction between the two masdar forms.

Abdel Haleem translates صَوْمًا more interpretively as "to abstain from conversation," which is contextually accurate but adds information not in the Arabic word itself.

Neither translation alerts the reader that the Quran uses two different words from the same root with distinct meanings.

Key Issue: Noun-to-Verb Conversion

Abdel Haleem frequently converts the noun صِيَام into the verb "to fast" or "he should fast." While this reads more naturally in English, it loses the Quran's deliberate choice to use the noun form. The Quran uses the noun صِيَام (9 times) far more than the verb (2 times), suggesting the concept-as-institution is more prominent than the act itself.

Recommendations

Best Translation for صِيَام Study

Sahih International is recommended for studying صِيَام because:

  • It correctly distinguishes صَوْم ("abstention") from صِيَام ("fasting")
  • It preserves the noun form rather than converting to verbs
  • It maintains the object pronoun in فَلْيَصُمْهُ ("let him fast it")
  • It stays closer to the Arabic grammatical structures

Abdel Haleem is useful for readability and capturing contextual meaning (e.g., "abstain from conversation" for صَوْمًا), but loses some morphological precision.

What Traditional Interpretations Miss

  1. The صِيَام/صَوْم distinction: The morphological difference between the two masdar forms is rarely highlighted. This is the most significant finding that traditional tafsirs generally overlook.
  2. The فِعَال pattern significance: The choice of the فِعَال pattern (sustained process) over the فَعْل pattern (basic act) for ritual fasting is not typically analyzed.
  3. The Ramadan-Quran priority: 2:185 introduces Ramadan through the Quran's revelation, not through fasting. Fasting is the response to this event, not its primary characteristic.
  4. The 33:35 structural position: Fasting as a bridge between external and internal virtues in the verse's sequence is an insight that emerges from careful positional analysis.
  5. The absence of direct imperative: The Quran never uses a direct "Fast!" command, always preferring indirect constructions. This suggests a relationship of invitation rather than compulsion.
