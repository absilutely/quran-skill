Section A: صِيَام (Masdar — فِعَال Pattern)

Ritual / Religious Fasting 9 occurrences in 7 verses

Verse 2:183 ٱلصِّيَامُ

يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
Sahih International: O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous.
Abdel Haleem: You who believe, fasting is prescribed for you, as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may be mindful of God.

Context: Opening verse of the fasting passage. ٱلصِّيَامُ appears as the subject (nominative, definite) — "the fasting" was prescribed. Note the passive verb كُتِبَ (was written/prescribed) — fasting is presented as a divine decree, not a human choice. Purpose stated: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ (that you may attain taqwa).

Verse 2:187 ٱلصِّيَامِ (×2)

أُحِلَّ لَكُمْ لَيْلَةَ ٱلصِّيَامِ ٱلرَّفَثُ إِلَىٰ نِسَآئِكُمْ ۚ هُنَّ لِبَاسٌ لَّكُمْ وَأَنتُمْ لِبَاسٌ لَّهُنَّ ۗ عَلِمَ ٱللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ كُنتُمْ تَخْتَانُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ وَعَفَا عَنكُمْ ۖ فَٱلْـَٰٔنَ بَـٰشِرُوهُنَّ وَٱبْتَغُوا۟ مَا كَتَبَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ ۚ وَكُلُوا۟ وَٱشْرَبُوا۟ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكُمُ ٱلْخَيْطُ ٱلْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ ٱلْخَيْطِ ٱلْأَسْوَدِ مِنَ ٱلْفَجْرِ ۖ ثُمَّ أَتِمُّوا۟ ٱلصِّيَامَ إِلَى ٱلَّيْلِ ۚ وَلَا تُبَـٰشِرُوهُنَّ وَأَنتُمْ عَـٰكِفُونَ فِى ٱلْمَسَـٰجِدِ ۗ تِلْكَ حُدُودُ ٱللَّهِ فَلَا تَقْرَبُوهَا ۗ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ ٱللَّهُ ءَايَـٰتِهِۦ لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَّقُونَ
Sahih International: It has been made permissible for you the night preceding fasting to go to your wives... and eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread [of night]. Then complete the fast until the night...
Abdel Haleem: You [believers] are permitted to lie with your wives during the night of the fast... eat and drink until you can distinguish a white thread from a black one at dawn, then fast until nightfall...

Context: Two occurrences: (1) لَيْلَةَ ٱلصِّيَامِ — "the night of the fasting" (genitive); (2) أَتِمُّوا۟ ٱلصِّيَامَ إِلَى ٱلَّيْلِ — "complete the fasting until the night" (accusative). This verse defines the temporal boundaries of صِيَام: from dawn to night. It also reveals what صِيَام entails: abstaining from food (كُلُوا۟), drink (ٱشْرَبُوا۟), and marital relations (ٱلرَّفَثُ) — all explicitly permitted at night, implying prohibition during the day.

Verse 2:196 صِيَامٍ + فَصِيَامُ

... فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَـٰثَةِ أَيَّامٍ فِى ٱلْحَجِّ وَسَبْعَةٍ إِذَا رَجَعْتُمْ ... فَمَن تَمَتَّعَ بِٱلْعُمْرَةِ إِلَى ٱلْحَجِّ فَمَا ٱسْتَيْسَرَ مِنَ ٱلْهَدْىِ ۚ فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَـٰثَةِ أَيَّامٍ فِى ٱلْحَجِّ وَسَبْعَةٍ إِذَا رَجَعْتُمْ ۗ تِلْكَ عَشَرَةٌ كَامِلَةٌ ...
Sahih International: ...whoever cannot find [or afford such an animal] - then a fast of three days during Hajj and seven when you have returned [home]. Those are ten complete [days]...
Abdel Haleem: ...If he lacks the means, he should fast for three days during the pilgrimage, and seven on his return, making ten days in all...

Context: Hajj context — fasting as alternative expiation (كفّارة) when unable to offer a sacrifice. فَصِيَامُ with the prefix فَ indicates a consequence/alternative. Fasting is quantified by specific days (three + seven = ten).

Verse 4:92 فَصِيَامُ

... فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ شَهْرَيْنِ مُتَتَابِعَيْنِ تَوْبَةً مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ عَلِيمًا حَكِيمًا
Sahih International: ...and whoever does not find [one or cannot afford to buy one] - then [instead], a fast for two months consecutively, [seeking] acceptance of repentance from Allah. And Allah is ever Knowing and Wise.
Abdel Haleem: ...if the offender does not have [a slave], he must fast for two consecutive months... as a way of showing repentance to God: God is all knowing, all wise.

Context: Expiation for accidental killing — fasting two consecutive months as alternative when freeing a slave is not possible. Note: fasting is described as تَوْبَةً مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ (a repentance from Allah / seeking Allah's acceptance) — linking fasting to spiritual purification.

Verse 5:89 فَصِيَامُ

... فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ ثَلَـٰثَةِ أَيَّامٍ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ كَفَّـٰرَةُ أَيْمَـٰنِكُمْ إِذَا حَلَفْتُمْ ...
Sahih International: ...But whoever cannot find [or afford it] - then a fast of three days [is required]. That is the expiation for oaths when you have sworn...
Abdel Haleem: ...if he cannot do this, he should fast for three days. This is the expiation for breaking your oaths...

Context: Expiation for broken oaths — three days of fasting as the final alternative. Explicitly called كَفَّـٰرَةُ (expiation/atonement).

Verse 5:95 صِيَامًا

... أَوْ عَدْلُ ذَٰلِكَ صِيَامًا لِّيَذُوقَ وَبَالَ أَمْرِهِۦ ...
Sahih International: ...or the equivalent of that in fasting, that he may taste the consequence of his deed...
Abdel Haleem: ...or he will fast an equivalent number of days, so that he may taste the consequences of his actions...

Context: Hunting violation during Hajj — fasting as expiation equivalent. Unique phrase: لِّيَذُوقَ وَبَالَ أَمْرِهِۦ (that he may taste the consequence of his deed) — fasting is explicitly linked to experiencing consequence, suggesting a pedagogical/disciplinary function.

Verse 58:4 فَصِيَامُ

فَمَن لَّمْ يَجِدْ فَصِيَامُ شَهْرَيْنِ مُتَتَابِعَيْنِ مِن قَبْلِ أَن يَتَمَآسَّا ۖ فَمَن لَّمْ يَسْتَطِعْ فَإِطْعَامُ سِتِّينَ مِسْكِينًا ...
Sahih International: And he who does not find [a slave] - then a fast for two months consecutively before they touch one another; and he who is unable - then the feeding of sixty poor persons...
Abdel Haleem: ...but anyone who does not have the means should fast continuously for two months before they touch each other, and anyone unable to do this should feed sixty needy people...

Context: Expiation for dhihār (ظِهَار) — two consecutive months of fasting. Note the tiered structure: freeing a slave → fasting → feeding the poor. Fasting occupies the middle tier of difficulty/severity.

Section B: صَوْم (Masdar — فَعْل Pattern)

General Abstention / Silence 1 occurrence

Verse 19:26 صَوْمًا

فَكُلِى وَٱشْرَبِى وَقَرِّى عَيْنًا ۖ فَإِمَّا تَرَيِنَّ مِنَ ٱلْبَشَرِ أَحَدًا فَقُولِىٓ إِنِّى نَذَرْتُ لِلرَّحْمَـٰنِ صَوْمًا فَلَنْ أُكَلِّمَ ٱلْيَوْمَ إِنسِيًّا
Sahih International: So eat and drink and be contented. And if you see from among humanity anyone, say, 'Indeed, I have vowed to the Most Merciful abstention, so I will not speak today to [any] man.'
Abdel Haleem: so eat, drink, be glad, and say to anyone you may see: "I have vowed to the Lord of Mercy to abstain from conversation, and I will not talk to anyone today."
CRITICAL FINDING: This is the ONLY verse where صَوْم (فَعْل pattern) is used instead of صِيَام (فِعَال pattern). Key observations:
  • Maryam is explicitly told to eat and drink (فَكُلِى وَٱشْرَبِى) — the opposite of food-fasting
  • The صَوْم here is defined by the verse itself: فَلَنْ أُكَلِّمَ ٱلْيَوْمَ إِنسِيًّا — "I will not speak to any human today"
  • This reveals that صَوْم means abstention in its broadest sense, while صِيَام is the specific religious practice

Verse 2:184 تَصُومُوا۟

أَيَّامًا مَّعْدُودَٰتٍ ۚ فَمَن كَانَ مِنكُم مَّرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ ۚ وَعَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُۥ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ ۖ فَمَن تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّهُۥ ۚ وَأَن تَصُومُوا۟ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ ۖ إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ
Sahih International: [Fasting for] a limited number of days. So whoever among you is ill or on a journey [during them] - then an equal number of days [are to be made up]... But to fast is best for you, if you only knew.
Abdel Haleem: Fast for a specific number of days, but if one of you is ill, or on a journey, on other days later... but it is better for you to fast, if only you knew.

Context: وَأَن تَصُومُوا۟ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ — "and that you fast is better for you." The verb تَصُومُوا (2nd person plural, jussive mood) is used in an evaluative statement — fasting is declared "better" (خَيْر) even when alternatives exist.

Verse 2:185 فَلْيَصُمْهُ

شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ هُدًى لِّلنَّاسِ وَبَيِّنَـٰتٍ مِّنَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ وَٱلْفُرْقَانِ ۚ فَمَن شَهِدَ مِنكُمُ ٱلشَّهْرَ فَلْيَصُمْهُ ۖ وَمَن كَانَ مَرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ ۗ يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ بِكُمُ ٱلْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ ٱلْعُسْرَ ...
Sahih International: The month of Ramadān [is that] in which was revealed the Qur'ān... So whoever sights [the crescent of] the month, let him fast it...
Abdel Haleem: It was in the month of Ramadan that the Quran was revealed... So any one of you who is present that month should fast...

Context: فَلْيَصُمْهُ — "let him fast it" — 3rd person imperative (jussive with لام الأمر) + object pronoun هُ referring to الشَّهْرَ (the month). This is the only instance where the verb takes an object, making fasting a transitive act directed at the month itself. The verse frames Ramadan through its connection to the Quran's revelation.

Section D: Active Participles (اسم الفاعل)

Character Trait / Identity 2 occurrences (1 verse)

Verse 33:35 وَٱلصَّـٰٓئِمِينَ + وَٱلصَّـٰٓئِمَـٰتِ

إِنَّ ٱلْمُسْلِمِينَ وَٱلْمُسْلِمَـٰتِ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَـٰتِ وَٱلْقَـٰنِتِينَ وَٱلْقَـٰنِتَـٰتِ وَٱلصَّـٰدِقِينَ وَٱلصَّـٰدِقَـٰتِ وَٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ وَٱلصَّـٰبِرَٰتِ وَٱلْخَـٰشِعِينَ وَٱلْخَـٰشِعَـٰتِ وَٱلْمُتَصَدِّقِينَ وَٱلْمُتَصَدِّقَـٰتِ وَٱلصَّـٰٓئِمِينَ وَٱلصَّـٰٓئِمَـٰتِ وَٱلْحَـٰفِظِينَ فُرُوجَهُمْ وَٱلْحَـٰفِظَـٰتِ وَٱلذَّٰكِرِينَ ٱللَّهَ كَثِيرًا وَٱلذَّٰكِرَٰتِ أَعَدَّ ٱللَّهُ لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةً وَأَجْرًا عَظِيمًا
Sahih International: Indeed, the Muslim men and Muslim women, the believing men and believing women, the obedient men and obedient women, the truthful men and truthful women, the patient men and patient women, the humble men and humble women, the charitable men and charitable women, the fasting men and fasting women, the men who guard their private parts and the women who do so, and the men who remember Allah often and the women who do so - for them Allah has prepared forgiveness and a great reward.
Abdel Haleem: For men and women who are devoted to God- believing men and women, obedient men and women, truthful men and women, steadfast men and women, humble men and women, charitable men and women, fasting men and women, chaste men and women, men and women who remember God often- God has prepared forgiveness and a rich reward.

Context: The active participles ٱلصَّـٰٓئِمِينَ وَٱلصَّـٰٓئِمَـٰتِ appear in a comprehensive list of 10 paired qualities. The فَاعِل pattern indicates a persistent characteristic, not a one-time action. Fasting (7th pair) is positioned between charity (المتصدقين) and guarding chastity (الحافظين فروجهم). This placement suggests fasting as a bridge between outward generosity and inward self-discipline. Reward: forgiveness + great reward (مَّغْفِرَةً وَأَجْرًا عَظِيمًا).
