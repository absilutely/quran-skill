Finding 1: Three Distinct Semantic Fields

The Quran uses root و س ع across three clearly distinct domains:

  1. Divine Encompassing (وَسِعَ / وَاسِع): Allah's knowledge, mercy, and throne encompass all things. Always Form I (stative).
  2. Human Capacity (وُسْع): The limit of what a soul can bear — لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا (2:286).
  3. Cosmic Expansion (مُوسِعُونَ): The sole Form IV active participle — وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ (51:47).

Finding 2: لَمُوسِعُونَ — Unique Form IV Usage

The word لَمُوسِعُونَ in 51:47 is the only Form IV active participle of this root in the entire Quran.

  • Form I (وَسِعَ): "it encompassed/was vast" — a state
  • Form IV (أَوْسَعَ): "to make vast/to expand" — a causative action

This is not "the heaven is vast" — it is "We are actively expanding the heaven." The active participle form indicates this is an ongoing process, not a completed event.

Finding 3: Remarkable Scientific Alignment

The Quran's morphologically precise statement — using Form IV causative active participle for the expansion of the heaven — aligns with the discovery of the expanding universe (Hubble, 1929), confirmed by CMB radiation (1965) and accelerating expansion (1998). The Quran's grammatical precision encodes active, ongoing expansion — exactly what modern cosmology observes.

Key Morphological Insight: Form I vs. Form IV

Form I (وَاسِعٌ): Describes a state of being vast. It's stative/descriptive — "His throne is vast."

Form IV (مُوسِعُونَ): Describes causing vastness. It's causative/active — "We are making it vast."

The Quran consistently uses Form I for existing attributes and Form IV only once — for the heaven. This distinction cannot be accidental.

4.2 The وَاسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ Pattern

The phrase وَٱللَّهُ وَٰسِعٌ عَلِيمٌ appears 7 times (2:115, 2:247, 2:261, 2:268, 3:73, 5:54, 24:32). It always follows mentions of Allah's giving, provision, or choice — suggesting that Allah's "vastness" is His unlimited capacity to give. His vastness operates through His knowledge.

4.3 The Structural Pairing of 51:47-48

The verse pairs two cosmic realities with distinct tenses:

  • Heaven: بَنَيْنَـٰهَا (past — We built it) + لَمُوسِعُونَ (ongoing — We are expanding it)
  • Earth: فَرَشْنَـٰهَا (past — We spread it) — completed action only

The heaven was built and continues to expand. The earth was spread and that action is complete. This tense shift within consecutive verses is structurally deliberate.

5.3 Why the Morphology Matters

The Quran had the tools to express simple vastness — it uses وَاسِعَة for the earth being "vast" (4:97, 29:56). But for the heaven, it chose a different morphological form entirely:

  • Earth = وَاسِعَة (Form I participle — it is vast, stative)
  • Heaven = لَمُوسِعُونَ (Form IV participle — We are making it vast, causative/ongoing)

If the intent were merely to describe the sky as vast, the Quran would have used the same Form I construction used for the earth. The deliberate shift to Form IV for the heaven — and only for the heaven — encodes an active, ongoing process of expansion.

Intellectual Honesty: Limitations

  • Classical scholars (pre-20th century) did not interpret لَمُوسِعُونَ as cosmic expansion. Their readings (ability, provision) were linguistically valid.
  • The Quran is primarily a book of guidance, not cosmology.
  • Retroactive scientific interpretation carries risks of confirmation bias.
  • However, the morphological precision is a textual fact — the Form IV causative is not Form I stative, regardless of interpretation.

6.2 Classical Tafsir Views

Ibn Kathir

"We made it vast and We brought its roof higher without pillars." Cites Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah for بِأَيْدٍ meaning "with strength." Interprets expansion as widening of sky's extent.

Partial — Captures vastness but treats it as completed.

Al-Qurtubi

Cites multiple views: (1) Ibn Abbas: "We are capable" (لَقادرون), (2) Ibn Abbas also: "We expand provision," (3) Ad-Dahhak: "We are wealthy," (4) Al-Hasan: "We are capable." Concludes: "The meanings are all close" (والمعنى متقارب).

Partial — Comprehensive but doesn't address cosmic expansion.

Al-Tabari (via Ibn Zayd)

Ibn Zayd stated simply: أوسعها جلّ جلاله — "He expanded it, Glorious is He." The pronoun هَا refers directly to the heaven.

High — The clearest classical statement of physical expansion.

Al-Sa'di

لَموسعون لأرجائها وأنحائها — "Expanders of its regions and directions." Also adds expansion of provision.

High — Primary meaning is physical expansion of the sky's regions.

6.3 Key Insight: Physical Expansion in Classical Tafsir

The physical expansion interpretation is not a modern invention. Both Al-Tabari (via Ibn Zayd: أوسعها — "He expanded it") and Al-Sa'di (لأرجائها وأنحائها — "its regions and directions") understood لَمُوسِعُونَ as referring to the physical expansion of the heaven. While not the majority classical view, this reading has legitimate roots in the tafsir tradition.
